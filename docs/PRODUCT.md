# Urbane — Product Brief

*Last updated: 2026-11-16. This replaces an earlier web-app brief drafted in ChatGPT ("NYC Homebase").*

## What it is

A private app for **Jon and Wendy** to pick a NYC rental (a pied-à-terre, 2BR/2BA) together. It is two
things at once, and it should feel like neither a spreadsheet nor a listings site:

- **A conceptualizer.** You browse possible lives: what the block looks like, the corner café, the
  walk to the train, what a Tuesday would be like.
- **A tracker.** Underneath, it's a shared shortlist with opinions, statuses and dated rent numbers,
  which eventually has to produce one answer.

Romance on top, honesty underneath. Every image comes from something we can show legitimately, and
every number shows its date and source.

## The loop: browse → fall for → narrow → watch → choose

1. **Browse.** A map of NYC with the neighborhoods we're considering, drawn as soft regions, and the
   buildings in them as pins. Each neighborhood is a **chapter**: a short essay in our own voice, a few
   street-level **vignettes**, the median 2BR rent and its trend, and how far it is from the places we
   go.
2. **Fall for.** Each building is a **postcard**. Swipe through its street-level views and an angled
   3D view of it on its block, plus a hero photo we chose ourselves. Then the rent band, the commute
   times, what renters say about the building and its landlord (ADR-0007), and our two reactions.
3. **Narrow.** Each of us rates and comments separately. A building's status is Exploring, Watching,
   Tour Candidate or Rejected. A rejected building keeps its reason, so we don't fall for it twice.
4. **Watch** *(later, M2)*. About two months before moving in, 2BR listings in the Watching buildings
   come in from StreetEasy alerts and are kept with their history.
5. **Choose** *(later)*. Visits, photos and a side-by-side comparison of the last two or three.

## What makes it browseable, not a launcher for Street View

The imagery is **generated by Apple frameworks on the device**, not scraped and not "tap here to go
look". See ADR-0002.

- **Look Around snapshots**: still images, so a building's gallery is just there to swipe.
- **An angled 3D map view** of each building in its block, taken from the same angle every time. This
  gives Urbane a consistent visual style.
- **One hero image per building**, picked by one of us from the building's own site with the in-app
  browser. A deliberate choice, never bulk collection.
- **Our own visit photos**, added to the same gallery later.

Live Look Around and Maps are still one tap away for wandering, but nothing depends on them.

## Words

- **Neighborhood chapters**: 150–250 words each, in our voice: what living there is like, not a real
  estate pitch. Drafted with Claude in chat, edited by us, stored as written (ADR-0004).
- **Import from chat** (ADR-0006): setup is a conversation in a Claude or ChatGPT project. When it's done, the chat
  returns one block that Urbane checks against Apple Maps, shows for review, and imports. That's how
  the neighborhoods, chapters and starting buildings get in, without typing "420 Kent" twenty times.
  The same paste can carry what renters say on Openigloo and elsewhere, each rating with its link
  and date (ADR-0007).
- **"A Tuesday here"**: for at most five buildings we love: morning coffee, the commute, dinner, an
  evening walk. Each stop gets a vignette. It's written by hand, and the other buildings simply don't
  have one.

## Numbers (ADR-0003)

- **Neighborhood 2BR median rent**, monthly, from StreetEasy's public data downloads.
- **Building rents**: recent 2BR rentals captured by hand from the building's StreetEasy page in the
  in-app browser. Each is a dated observation.
- **Commute times** by transit to SoHo, Tribeca, West Village, Lower East Side and Midtown, calculated
  once per building and stored.

## Starting set

8–10 neighborhoods and 15–20 buildings. The first list: Williamsburg, Downtown Brooklyn, Lincoln
Square/UWS, Chelsea/NoMad, Long Island City. Jon and Wendy pick the buildings.

## Success

Within a few minutes, either of us can open a neighborhood, get a feel for a building, react to it
and see what the other thinks. When we sit down to decide, the comparison is already in the app,
not in our heads.

## Out of scope

- A web client, a server, an App Store release.
- Any automated collection of listing photos, or scraping of rents.
- A comprehensive NYC database. Urbane only knows buildings we added.
- AI generation inside the app, and recommendation features of any kind. (The chat drafts; we review
  and import.)
- Apartment lines and stacks, the visit itinerary, the comparison view and listing alerts are
  **not in M1**. They're parked in `open-questions.md` until their time comes.
- Anything we'd still be maintaining after the lease is signed.
