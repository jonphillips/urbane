# M1 — Browse: neighborhoods, postcards, and two opinions

**Status:** Designed · **Summary:** the conceptualizer and the shortlist, shared between Jon and Wendy.
No listings yet.
**Authority:** ADR-0001 to ADR-0005. Read the ADRs first; this file turns them into dispatches.

**The governing restraint:** M1 is done when the two of us can browse, react and narrow. It does not
include a comparison view, visits, listings, or anything generated by AI.

## Constants

- Bundle `com.jonphillips.urbane`, container `iCloud.com.jonphillips.urbane`, app group
  `group.com.jonphillips.urbane` (a share extension is likely later, so put the database in the app
  group from the start, per jon-platform § Capture into a shared container).

- Commute destinations: five fixed coordinates in code (ADR-0005).

## Slices

### S1 — Scaffold and the imagery proof ← build first

The project, the `UrbaneLibrary` package, the schema migration for every M1 table (ADR-0005), local-only
storage, and **the proof the product depends on**: a debug screen listing about eight seeded NYC
addresses (a list Jon supplies in the brief or the PR), each showing its Look Around snapshot from an
`MKMapItem` request and the angled 3D snapshot.
**Done when:** the gate passes, and Jon looks at the debug screen on his iPad and answers ADR-0002's two
open questions in the PR. This is a device gate. If the answer is "not good enough", stop before S2.

### S2 — The map and adding buildings

A full-screen map with neighborhoods as soft regions and buildings as pins colored by status. Add a
neighborhood by drawing or dropping a region center and span. Add a building with `MKLocalSearch` by
name or address, or by pasting a StreetEasy or building-site URL (take the name and address from the
URL slug, then search for it). The StreetEasy URL is stored, never fetched.
**Done when:** seeded neighborhoods and buildings appear on the map, and the URL slug parser is
unit-tested on real URL shapes.

### S3 — The building postcard

A building detail screen: a swipeable gallery (3D snapshot first, then the facade and corner Look Around
stills, then the hero image), name, address, status, and a placeholder for the rent band. Building
vignettes can be added and reordered. The snapshot cache is local, keyed by a hash of the vignette's
recipe. Choose the hero image with the in-app browser through `WebExtractorKit`'s capture, then downscale it.
**Done when:** a building with no hero image and no Look Around coverage still looks good. Device gate:
Jon swipes through five real buildings.

### S4 — Neighborhood chapters

A neighborhood screen: the chapter text (editable as a plain multi-line field), 3–5 vignettes, its
buildings, and the rent placeholder. Tapping a neighborhood region on the map opens it.
**Done when:** a chapter pasted from a chat keeps its paragraphs exactly as written (ADR-0004).

### S5 — Rent numbers and commutes

The neighborhood CSV importer (fails loudly on an unknown header) and sparkline. Building rent capture:
browser page → `LLMClientKit` extraction → a review sheet → saved observations. Rent bands and
date-and-source labels everywhere a rent appears (ADR-0003). Commute times are calculated once per building
per destination with `MKDirections` ETAs by transit, stored, and refreshable by hand.
**Done when:** extraction is tested against saved fixtures of real StreetEasy pages, and no
observation is saved without a confirm step.

### S6 — Two opinions, sharing on

The `people` table and the "this is me" binding, following Galavant ADR-0051. Per-person reactions,
status changes with a required rejection reason, and a filter for Rejected. Then turn on sync and
sharing, following jon-platform § Creating and accepting the share, plus CloudSyncKit's sync health
and backup.
**Done when:** the gate passes and the schema is imported with `cktool`. **Escalated to Jon** (sync,
sharing, Production schema). Device gate: Wendy accepts the share on her iPad, and each of us sees the
other's reaction within a minute.

### S7 — "A Tuesday here"

The ordered building vignettes with `tuesdayPart` set, shown as a vertical story on the postcard:
time-of-day label, line of text, still image, and the commute stop's stored time. It only appears when
the building has at least one Tuesday stop.
**Done when:** Jon writes one for a real building and it reads well on the iPad.
