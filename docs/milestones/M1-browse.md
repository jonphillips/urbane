# M1 — Browse: neighborhoods, postcards, and two opinions

**Status:** Designed · **Summary:** the conceptualizer and the shortlist, shared between Jon and Wendy,
seeded from a chat. No listings yet.
**Authority:** ADR-0001 to ADR-0008. Read the ADRs first; this file turns them into dispatches.

**The governing restraint:** M1 is done when the two of us can browse, react and narrow. It does not
include a comparison view, visits, listings, or anything generated by AI inside the app.

## Constants

- Bundle `com.jonphillips.urbane`, container `iCloud.com.jonphillips.urbane`, app group
  `group.com.jonphillips.urbane` (a share extension is likely later, so put the database in the app
  group from the start, per jon-platform § Capture into a shared container).

- Chat contract marker: `URBANE-CONTRACT: v1` (ADR-0006, `docs/chat-contract.md`).

## Slices

### S1 — Scaffold and the imagery proof ← build first

The project, the `UrbaneLibrary` package, the schema migration for every M1 table (ADR-0005, including
`buildings.summary`), local-only storage, and **the proof the product depends on**: a debug screen listing
about eight seeded NYC addresses (a list Jon supplies in the brief or the PR), each showing its Look Around
snapshot from an `MKMapItem` request and the angled 3D snapshot. Also the house CI and hooks: `ci.yml`
calling `ci-workflows` with `package-path: UrbaneLibrary`, and the tracked `.githooks/pre-commit`
(jon-platform `drift-control.md` § Starting a new app).
**Done when:** the gate passes, CI is green on the PR, and Jon looks at the debug screen on his iPad and answers ADR-0002's two
open questions in the PR. This is a device gate. If the answer is "not good enough", stop before S2.

### S2 — The map and adding buildings by hand

A full-screen map with neighborhoods as soft regions and buildings as pins colored by status. Add a
neighborhood by name (its region from a search, adjustable by hand). Add a building with `MKLocalSearch` by
name or address, or by pasting a StreetEasy or building-site URL (take the name and address from the URL
slug, then search for it). The StreetEasy URL is stored, never fetched. When search finds nothing (420
Kent Avenue in S1), place the pin by hand: the address as typed, no map item (ADR-0002 § Resolved by S1).
Every building has **Fix location**, which re-runs the search or moves the pin. The place-search code is a
dependency that S3 reuses.
**Done when:** neighborhoods and buildings appear on the map, the URL slug parser is unit-tested on
real URL shapes, and Jon adds 420 Kent Avenue on the iPad, by search or by pin.

### S3 — Import from chat

ADR-0006, all of it: a paste sheet, marker check, strict decode, on-device resolution of every building,
neighborhood and vignette, the bulk review (new, already in Urbane, unresolved; chapter keep/replace), and
a single-transaction write that fills only empty fields. Plus ADR-0007's reviews: a second migration adding
`buildingReviews`, the strict review decode, and review rows in the bulk review (new, or old → new for a
replacement). And a third migration adding `buildings.mapItemIdentifier`
(ADR-0005), which the identifier-first match reads.
**Done when:** decoding, matching (by map item identifier, then normalized address) and "fill only
empty fields" are unit-tested in `UrbaneLibrary` against fixture pastes. The fixtures include a missing
marker, a newer marker, an unknown status, a rejected building with no reason, and a re-import over
edited data. Review fixtures cover a rating without a scale, a rating above its scale, an unknown
`about`, a missing URL, and a re-import that replaces one review and keeps another. Device gate: Jon pastes
one real seed from a Claude or ChatGPT project using `docs/chat-contract.md`, with Openigloo reviews for at
least one building.

### S4 — The building postcard

A building detail screen: a swipeable gallery (3D snapshot first, then the facade and corner Look Around
stills, then the hero image), name, address, summary, status, a placeholder for the rent band, and "What renters say": each
`buildingReviews` row with its source, subject, rating, count and dates, muted when stale, never averaged
(ADR-0007).
Building vignettes can be added and reordered. The snapshot cache is local, keyed by a hash of the vignette's
recipe. Choose the hero image with the in-app browser through `WebExtractorKit`'s capture, then downscale it.
Tapping any gallery page opens it full screen: the 3D view as a live map, a Look Around still in the
Look Around viewer. Views aren't adjustable (ADR-0002 § Resolved by S4).
**Done when:** a building with no hero image and no Look Around coverage still looks good. Device gate:
Jon swipes through five real buildings and looks around from at least one still.

### S5 — Neighborhood chapters

A neighborhood screen: the chapter text (editable as a plain multi-line field), 3–5 vignettes, its
buildings, and the rent placeholder. Tapping a neighborhood region on the map opens it.

Plus three map fixes from Jon's first real seed (2026-11-16):
- **Smaller regions.** Import gives every neighborhood a fixed 0.06° span, about a 3 km circle, so 11
  neighborhoods blanket the map. Import and Add neighborhood both default to 0.02°.
- **Fix location for a neighborhood**, on its screen, as buildings have: re-run the search, or set the
  center and span by hand. "Fulton / Seaport" resolved to Brooklyn's Fulton Street.
- **One label per neighborhood.** The map shows each name twice, because the `Annotation` title renders
  under the custom label. Hide the title.

**Done when:** an imported chapter keeps its paragraphs exactly as written (ADR-0004), and a
neighborhood's region can be moved and resized from its screen.

### S6 — Rent numbers and commutes

The neighborhood CSV importer (fails loudly on an unknown header) and sparkline. Building rent capture:
browser page → `LLMClientKit` extraction → a review sheet → saved observations. Rent bands and
date-and-source labels everywhere a rent appears (ADR-0003). Commute times are calculated once per building
per destination with `MKDirections` ETAs by transit, stored, and refreshable by hand. A building added by
import gets its commutes calculated in the background.
**Done when:** extraction is tested against saved fixtures of real StreetEasy pages, and no
observation is saved without a confirm step.

### S7 — The subway on the map

ADR-0008, added at Jon's request (2026-11-16). The subway has to be the most obvious thing on the
map, because without easy transit no building is worth having.

- **Station data.** The MTA snapshot as a JSON resource in `UrbaneLibrary`, with its source URL and
  fetch date. A pure loader groups rows by `complex_id` into station complexes, each with a name (the
  stop name most of its rows share, otherwise the first), the union of its daytime routes in MTA
  order, and the mean coordinate.
- **Line colors, from the MTA's palette.** 1 2 3 red `#EE352E`; 4 5 6 green `#00933C`; 7 purple
  `#B933AD`; A C E blue `#0039A6`; B D F M orange `#FF6319`; G lime `#6CBE45`; J Z brown `#996633`;
  L grey `#A7A9AC`; N Q R W yellow `#FCCC0A`, with black text; S and SIR dark grey `#808183`. A route
  that isn't in the table fails a test, never a silent grey.
- **Markers on both maps:** the home map, and the full-screen live 3D map you open from a building's
  postcard. Each complex is a bold marker with a round bullet per route in the line's color, sized to
  read at a glance in realistic 3D. Its name shows once zoomed in. Draw only the complexes in the
  visible region, and only below a zoom threshold, so a borough-wide view isn't a carpet of dots.
  Hide Apple's transit points of interest on both maps (`pointsOfInterest: .excluding([.publicTransport])`),
  so nothing appears twice.
- **Nearest stations on the building sheet.** A "Subway" section lists the three nearest complexes
  within 800 m. Each shows its route bullets, name, and straight-line distance as a walk ("≈ 6 min
  walk · straight line", at 80 m per minute). If there are none, it says "No subway within 800 m".
  It's computed from the bundled list, with no MapKit requests.

**Done when:** `UrbaneLibrary` tests cover complex grouping (Times Sq-42 St carries 1 2 3 7 A C E N
Q R S W), a color for every route in the snapshot, and nearest-station ordering and cutoff. Device
gate: on the iPad, Jon can pick out the stations and their lines on the 3D map around Union Square
and Williamsburg without hunting, and a real building's sheet lists the stations he'd actually use.

### S7b — Fixes from the S6 and S7 device pass

Jon's iPad pass on 2026-11-17 found two problems.

**1. Rent capture finds nothing on a real page.** On 420 Kent's building page, showing its 2BR
units, Extract rents says "No 2 bedroom rental candidates were found". The page-text reducer isn't
the cause; the on-device model is. Run on this Mac against the two real fixtures, with today's
plain-text request, it answers `[]` for both pages. With guided generation (a JSON schema), and
without the example JSON in the instructions, it returned every unit with the right bedroom count
on each of three runs. That's all 12 units on the building page and #560 at $7,482 on the listing.
The example JSON matters: with it left in, the model copied it back (`1F`, $4,395) on the listing
page.

- **Ask for every unit, then filter in code.** Send `ModelRequest(responseFormat: .jsonSchema(…))`,
  which `OnDeviceModelClient` already turns into a `GenerationSchema`. The schema is an object with
  one required array, `listings` ("Every rental unit currently listed on the page"). Each item is an
  object with three required properties:
  - `bedrooms` (integer): "Bedrooms, from text like 2 beds; 0 for a studio. Never the rooms count."
  - `rent` (integer): "Monthly base rent in dollars"
  - `unit` (string): "Unit number, e.g. 1009"
- **Replace the instructions with exactly this text**, which contains no example values:
  > You read the visible text of a StreetEasy rental page. List every rental unit the page
  > currently offers: its unit number (as written after #), its base rent, and its bedrooms from
  > "N beds" or "N bed" (0 for a studio; "rooms" is not bedrooms). Use only numbers shown on the
  > page. Skip price history, neighborhood medians, and similar or nearby homes. If no unit is
  > offered, return an empty list.
- **Keep only `bedrooms == 2` and `rent > 0`,** and strip a leading `#` and whitespace from the unit.
  Parse strictly. An answer that doesn't decode is an error that says so, not an empty list.
- **Say what was found.** When there are no 2BR candidates, the message names what the model saw:
  "Found 9 units on this page, none with 2 bedrooms", or "No rental units found on this page".
- **Copy page text.** After a capture with no candidates, the rent section offers "Copy page text",
  which puts on the clipboard exactly the reduced text that was sent to the model. The iPad gets
  StreetEasy's mobile layout, while our fixtures are the desktop one, so this is how Jon hands us
  the mobile page as a fixture if the fix isn't enough there.

**2. Subway markers: bigger close up, and visible from higher up.** Jon: "50% bigger when I'm very
zoomed in" and "visible at a higher altitude". Today markers appear only below a 4,440 m camera
distance, at a fixed 21 pt bullet.

- **Show markers up to a 10,000 m camera distance.** Jon's Midtown-to-Long Island City view showed
  none.
- **Scale with camera distance.** The bullet diameter, the bullet font and the marker padding are
  1.5× today's size at 600 m or closer and 1.0× at 4,440 m or farther, linear in between. The name
  threshold (1,500 m) is unchanged.
- **Put the scale in `UrbaneCore`** as a pure `SubwayMarkerScale.scale(cameraDistance:)` with tests:
  1.5 at 600 m and below, 1.0 at 4,440 m and above, and a value strictly between at 2,000 m. Put
  the 10,000 m cutoff next to it.
- If continuous resizing stutters with a lot of markers, round the scale to steps of 0.1.

**Done when:** tests cover the request's schema and instructions (no `4395` or `1F` in them), the
2BR filter and unit normalization on a structured answer, an undecodable answer becoming an error,
the existing real-fixture reducer tests, and the marker scale. Device gate: Capture from
StreetEasy on 420 Kent's building page offers #560, #802 and #1009 and nothing else. Subway
markers show at the altitude of Jon's Midtown/LIC screenshot, and are about half again as big
fully zoomed in.

### S7c — Two touches from the S7b pass

Jon's iPad pass on 2026-11-17: rent capture and the subway markers work. Two small asks came out of it.

1. **Point at the 2 beds tab.** On the iPad, StreetEasy's building page shows one bedroom tab at a
   time, and its default tab (Studio) is the only one the capture can read. Selecting **2 beds**
   first makes capture work. Don't try to read hidden tabs. Instead, when units were found but none
   with 2 bedrooms, the message becomes: "Found N units on this page, none with 2 bedrooms. If the
   page has a 2 beds tab, tap it, then Extract rents again." The other messages stay as they are.
2. **Transit directions from a commute row.** Each row in "Commutes by transit" becomes a button,
   with a trailing `arrow.up.right` and an accessibility hint of "Opens transit directions in Maps".
   Tapping it calls `MKMapItem.openMaps(with: [origin, destination], launchOptions:
   [MKLaunchOptionsDirectionsModeKey: MKLaunchOptionsDirectionsModeTransit])`.
   - The origin is the building's own map item (`MKMapItemRequest` with its `mapItemIdentifier`)
     when it has one. Otherwise, or if that request fails, it's a map item at the building's
     coordinate named after the building.
   - The destination is a map item at the `CommuteDestination` coordinate, named by its
     `displayName`.
   - Use MapKit only (ADR-0002). Don't build a URL to Apple Maps.

**Done when:** a test covers the new no-2BR message (which depends on the count), and the gate
passes. Device gate: on 420 Kent's building page, the Studio tab shows the new message; tapping
a commute row opens Apple Maps with transit directions from the building to that destination.

### S8a — Two opinions, on one device

The opinion half of the old S8, with sync still off. It needs no schema change: `people` and `reactions`
have existed since S1's migration.

- **People.** Seed `people` rows for Jon and Wendy (`displayName` only, under the one `searches` root).
  "This is me" is a **device-local** choice (a `UserDefaults` key, not synced), picked once in Settings
  and shown there. This is the interim shape jon-platform `persistence-and-sync.md` allows; S8b binds
  it to the iCloud account (`iCloudUserRecordName`, Galavant ADR-0051).
- **Per-person reactions.** On the building sheet, "me" can set a rating (1–5), a favorite, and a note.
  It's one `reactions` row per (building, person), updated in place and never duplicated. Both
  people's reactions are shown, labelled by `displayName`. With no "this is me" chosen, the controls
  say to pick one in Settings.
- **Status with a required reason.** The building's status can be changed on its sheet. Moving to
  Rejected requires a non-empty `rejectionReason`, and leaving Rejected clears it.
- **A Rejected filter on the map.** Rejected buildings are hidden by default, with a toggle to show them.

**Done when:** `UrbaneLibrary` tests cover the reaction upsert (one row per pair, edits in place),
the rejection-reason rule, and the filter. **Not escalated:** no schema, sync or sharing change. If
the slice finds it needs a migration, stop and say so in the PR. Device gate: Jon sets a reaction as
himself, switches "this is me" to Wendy, sets hers, and sees both on the sheet.

### S8b — Sync and sharing on, through Production

The sync half of the old S8. **Escalated to Jon** (sync, sharing, Production schema). The order,
and who does each step, is [PROD-CUTOVER.md](../PROD-CUTOVER.md). Read it before starting. In short:

- iCloud and CloudKit entitlements, `CKSharingSupported`, and the remote-notification background mode.
- A `SyncEngine` through `CloudSyncKit`, started with `startImmediately: false` behind a persisted
  opt-in and an account check. Sync health and backup/restore in Settings.
- **The patched SQLiteData.** Jon's library has never synced, so its first upload goes into an empty
  Production zone. Unpatched SQLiteData deletes single-parent children when they reach the server
  before their parents (jon-platform `SEAM-LEDGER.md`, "Patched SQLiteData"; Yes Chef lost 669 rows
  that way). Use Yes Chef's vendored copy as a local-package override, as Yes Chef does.
- Share create (owner only) and accept (both scene paths), following jon-platform § Creating and
  accepting the share and Galavant's code. "This is me" binds to the iCloud user per Galavant
  ADR-0051; read its open-question answers first.
- `testflight.conf` with `SCHEMA_GATE=1` and `EXPECTED_SCHEMA_FIELDS`, and the full CloudKit schema
  made with `cktool` (§ Adding a synced table or column).
- **One root per device, after accepting.** Today `bootstrap` creates a `searches` row on first launch,
  and S8a seeds Jon and Wendy under it with random IDs. On Wendy's fresh install, that gives her a
  second root, with its own Jon and Wendy, before she accepts the share. After accepting, the app
  would hold two roots and four people, and `searches.first` would pick either. Fix it like this:
  - **The active search** is the shared root when one exists, otherwise the local one. Every
    `searches.first` read goes through one `activeSearch` accessor.
  - **On accepting a share,** delete the local, never-shared root if it holds no neighborhoods or
    buildings. The cascade removes its seeded people. If it does hold data, stop and tell the user,
    rather than merging two libraries.
  - **Seed people only under a root this device owns.** A participant never seeds; it uses the
    owner's people. "This is me" then binds to one of those rows (Galavant ADR-0051).
  - Test the root choice and the empty-root cleanup in `UrbaneLibrary`. Acceptance itself is the
    device gate.

**Done when:** the gate passes; the schema is imported to Development with `cktool` and matches
`EXPECTED_SCHEMA_FIELDS`; and the PR lists the PROD-CUTOVER steps that are Jon's. Device gate (the
held sharing pass): after the cutover, Wendy accepts the share on her own iPad from TestFlight; each
of us sees the other's reaction within a minute; and every table's row count matches between
Jon's and Wendy's backups.
